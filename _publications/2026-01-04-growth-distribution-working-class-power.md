**Abstract:**
This paper develops a dynamic account of working-class power using a Goodwin-type growth-distribution framework. It distinguishes structural power (labour-market tightness) from associational power (organization/coordination) and embeds them into the wage-setting relation of a Goodwin model. We introduce a stability lever that depends on union structure and show how institutional configurations shift the system between damped and persistent distributive cycles via a Hopf bifurcation. The paper is operationalized using long-run Chilean data on wage shares, employment, and union structure, providing a formal but accessible framework for analyzing how organization shapes distributive conflict over time.

**Key contributions:**
- A typology of working-class power as structural and associational.
- A disciplined extension of the Goodwin model that integrates organization into wage-setting dynamics.
- A regime interpretation of distributive cycles based on local stability and the Hopf boundary.
- Empirical operationalization using historical Chilean data.
